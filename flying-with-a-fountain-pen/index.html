Flying With a Fountain Pen: How to Avoid Leaks at Takeoff, and How to Pack Ink

Why fountain pens can leak as cabin pressure changes, and how to prevent it: empty or full, nib up, don't write during the climb, and pack ink bottles carefully.

A fountain pen that behaves perfectly at your desk can leave a blot on your shirt after a flight. That isn’t a defect. It’s air pressure. The good news is that prevention is simple and costs nothing. This guide explains what happens and gives a short packing routine.

It covers practical pen care only. Airline and airport rules on liquids and carry-on items vary by country and change over time. Check your airline and the relevant security authority before you pack ink.

Why fountain pens leak on planes

Goulet Pens explains the mechanism clearly. As the plane climbs, cabin pressure drops, so the air trapped inside your pen’s ink chamber is at a higher pressure than the air around it. That air “wants to equalize,” and the only way out is through the nib. If there’s ink between the trapped air and the nib, the ink gets pushed out first (Goulet Pens).

Two things follow from that:

  1. The more air in the reservoir, the more there is to expand. A half-empty pen is the riskiest kind (Goulet Pens).
  2. The climb after takeoff is the risky window. Goulet describes the biggest risk as takeoff, when pressure is dropping. During descent pressure rises, which pushes ink back into the pen rather than out (Goulet Pens).

Repair specialist Richard Binder gives the same practical warning: don’t use your pens “during the period when your plane is climbing to altitude,” because they are “much more likely to spit, blob, or flood then” (Richard Binder).

The four rules

1. Empty is safest

Goulet’s first suggestion: clean pens and keep them empty before flying. It’s “the only 100% guaranteed way to avoid an ink spill” (Goulet Pens). Binder recommends inking only the one or two pens you’ll actually use while traveling, and traveling with the rest empty (Richard Binder). Our cleaning guide covers the flush.

2. If inked, make it full

If you do carry an inked pen, fill it completely. Goulet explains that trouble comes from “some ink and a lot of air,” so keeping pens as full as possible reduces the risk (Goulet Pens). Binder also says to fill carry pens completely (Richard Binder).

3. Keep the nib pointing up

Store inked pens nib-up in your bag or pocket. Goulet explains that this keeps any air bubble near the nib, so expanding air escapes without pushing ink out (Goulet Pens). Binder gives the same advice: carry pens “nib upward when they’re not in use” (Richard Binder).

4. Don’t write during the climb

Wait until the plane has finished climbing before uncapping. When you open the pen, hold it nib-up for a moment first, and have a tissue ready.

Contain the worst case

Even careful packing can fail. Goulet suggests putting pens in a zip-lock bag so any leak is contained (Goulet Pens). A plain pen case with the pens standing nib-up inside a sealed bag is a cheap, low-effort setup.

For pens traveling empty, Binder suggests a strong pen case, or wrapping pens in tissue and packing them in rigid tubes so they don’t rattle (Richard Binder).

Packing ink bottles

Bottles behave differently from pens. Goulet notes that ink bottles are generally fine with pressure, because the pressure difference equalizes as soon as you open the cap. They suggest worrying more about physical damage from baggage handling and packing bottles well (Goulet Pens).

Binder recommends sealing any travel ink in a zip-lock bag and packing it in your carry-on, warning that glass bottles don’t survive drops well in checked luggage (Richard Binder). That advice predates many current carry-on liquid rules, so check what your airport allows before packing ink in hand luggage.

Practical options:

  • Bring sample vials instead of full bottles. Pen Weekly suggests filling vials to just below the neck, labeling them, and wrapping the cap threads with tape or lab film for anything you plan to move (Pen Weekly).
  • Double-bag anything liquid. A second sealed bag costs nothing.
  • Use cartridges. If your pen takes them, spare sealed cartridges are often the simplest travel ink. Check that they fit your specific pen.

On arrival

  • Uncap carefully, nib-up, with a tissue around the section the first time.
  • If the nib is wet or blobby, wipe it and write a few lines on scrap paper.
  • If a pen leaked into its cap, flush the pen and rinse the cap with water once you’ve arrived, then dry both.
  • Check bottles and vials for seepage before setting them on hotel furniture.

Common questions

Should I put my pens in checked luggage?

For inked pens, carry-on is the better choice, because you can keep them upright and deal with a leak straight away. For empty pens, either works if they’re well protected. Binder’s suggestion of a strong case or rigid tubes, with pens wrapped so they don’t rattle, applies in both cases (Richard Binder). For glass ink bottles, Binder warns that checked bags can be dropped hard (Richard Binder). Weigh that against your airport’s carry-on liquid rules before deciding.

Does this only happen on planes?

The mechanism Goulet describes is a pressure difference between the air inside the pen and the air outside (Goulet Pens). Any situation where outside pressure drops while the pen is partly full works the same way in principle. That’s why the same habits help: keep pens full or empty, store them nib-up, and don’t uncap them while pressure is changing. Warmth can also make a nearly empty pen behave unpredictably, so keep pens out of hot cars and direct sun as you would ink bottles (Pen Weekly).

My pen leaked anyway. Is it damaged?

Usually not. A burp of ink into the cap is a mess, not a failure. Flush the pen and rinse the cap with clean water (Goulet Pens — cleaning), let both dry, and refill. If the pen keeps leaking at your desk, with no change in pressure, the problem may be a cracked part or a poorly seated converter. Work through our converter guide or contact the manufacturer.

Which pen should I bring?

The one you’d least mind getting messy, already flushed and filled. A trip is a poor time to break in a new pen or try an unfamiliar ink. Bring a pen and ink combination you already know behaves well, so any problem on arrival is easy to diagnose.

A one-minute pre-flight checklist

  1. Flush and empty any pen you won’t use on the trip.
  2. Fill the one or two carry pens completely.
  3. Store them nib-up, inside a zip-lock bag.
  4. Don’t write during the climb.
  5. Pack ink as vials or cartridges, double-bagged, after checking current carry-on rules.
